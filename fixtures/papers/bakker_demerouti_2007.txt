TITLE: The job demands-resources model: state of the art
REFERENCE: Bakker, A. B. & Demerouti, E. (2007). Journal of Managerial Psychology, 22(3), 309-328.

DEFINITIONS
- Job demands: physical, psychological, social or organizational aspects of the job that require sustained physical and/or psychological effort and are associated with physiological and/or psychological costs (p. 312). Examples: work pressure, emotional demands, role conflict.
- Job resources: aspects of the job that are functional in achieving work goals, reduce job demands and their costs, or stimulate personal growth, learning and development (p. 312). Examples: autonomy, social support, performance feedback, opportunities for development.
- Work engagement: a positive, fulfilling work-related state of mind (vigor, dedication, absorption), after Schaufeli et al. (2002).
- Exhaustion: the core burnout dimension, depletion of energy.

PROPOSITIONS
1. Job demands are the most important predictors of exhaustion (health impairment process). Evidence: multiple cross-sectional and longitudinal studies reviewed.
2. Job resources are the most important predictors of work engagement (motivational process).
3. Job resources buffer the impact of job demands on exhaustion (buffer hypothesis; Bakker, Demerouti & Euwema 2005).
4. Job resources particularly influence engagement when job demands are high (Bakker, Hakanen, Demerouti & Xanthopoulou 2007).
5. Engagement predicts in-role and extra-role performance.

MEASUREMENT: self-report questionnaires at the individual level; the model is tested with cross-sectional and multi-wave designs.
