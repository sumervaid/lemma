TITLE: Conservation of resources: a new attempt at conceptualizing stress
REFERENCE: Hobfoll, S. E. (1989). American Psychologist, 44(3), 513-524.

DEFINITIONS
- Resources: objects, personal characteristics, conditions, or energies that are valued by the individual or that serve as a means for attainment of other valued resources (p. 516).
- Resource loss: actual loss of resources; threat of loss; and lack of gain following investment of resources.
- Psychological stress: a reaction to the environment in which there is threat of net loss, actual net loss, or lack of resource gain following investment.

PROPOSITIONS
1. Resource loss produces psychological stress (strain). Loss is more salient than gain.
2. People invest resources to protect against loss, recover from loss and gain resources; those with more resources are less vulnerable to loss and more capable of gain.
3. Loss begets further loss (loss spirals); gain begets further gain (gain spirals).

MEASUREMENT: the paper is conceptual; it reviews studies of stressful life events and proposes a resource-based measurement approach at the individual level.
