TITLE: A socially embedded model of thriving at work
REFERENCE: Spreitzer, G., Sutcliffe, K., Dutton, J., Sonenshein, S. & Grant, A. M. (2005). Organization Science, 16(5), 537-549.

DEFINITIONS
- Thriving: the psychological state in which individuals experience both a sense of vitality and a sense of learning at work (p. 538).
- Vitality: the positive feeling of having energy available (p. 539).
- Learning: the sense that one is acquiring, and can apply, knowledge and skills (p. 538).
- Agentic work behaviors: task focus, exploration, heedful relating (pp. 540-542).
- Unit contextual features: decision-making discretion, broad information sharing, climate of trust and respect (pp. 542-543).

PROPOSITIONS (theoretical, no data)
P1-P3. Task focus, exploration and heedful relating each promote thriving.
P4-P6. Decision-making discretion, broad information sharing and a climate of trust and respect each promote agentic work behaviors.
P7. Thriving promotes individual development.
P8. Thriving promotes health.

LEVEL: individual thriving embedded in a work unit; the paper is conceptual and proposes no horizon.
